Method

What this model is, what it takes in, what it gives back — and, in the same breath, what it does not contain.

What the model is
  • A deterministic cohort simulation over 60 months. No randomness, no probability, no fitted parameters.
  • Company ARR is only ever the sum of its cohorts. Aggregates are never asserted; they are summed.
  • The same engine runs the browser and the command-line checks — one source of truth, 35 integrity checks.
What is input
  • Management decisions — S&M, and the fixed operating costs R&D and G&A.
  • Economic transition assumptions — persistence and expansion (how the installed base evolves), CAC per €1 of New ARR (how spend becomes ARR), gross margin (how revenue becomes gross profit).
  • Three nullable mechanisms — expansion realisation cost, acquisition capacity, acquisition lag. Each defaults to the setting under which it does nothing.
  • Customer physics (v2, nullable) — logo retention, contraction and ARR per new logo. When on, persistence is derived from them, never set.
  • Monetization physics (v2, nullable) — a platform fee and a usage component per customer, with price growth, usage growth and adoption to caps. When on, revenue is derived from the components; the expansion coefficient, ARR per new logo and opening ARR are not read.
  • Cash physics (v2, nullable) — billing term and timing (deferred revenue or contract asset) and a collection delay (receivables). When on, cash FCF = collections − cash costs; EBITA is untouched.
  • Hypotheses (v2, none by default) — a costed, lagged, temporary move of one law, resolved into the law in force month by month. Never a coefficient.
  • Nothing else. There is no plan, no target, no forecast to hit.
What is output
  • ARR and its cohort composition, revenue, gross profit, EBITA, FCF, cash.
  • R12M GRR, expansion and NRR — measured, never entered.
  • Coefficient payback, an output of CAC coefficient × 12 ÷ GM. In the first version it was an input; inverting that was the most important correction this project has made. Under a capacity the product also shows average and marginal payback (the same construction on average and marginal CAC); the capital track shows each cohort's realised payback. Every CAC is € of S&M per €1 of ARR, in either display basis.
Reporting basis — MRR ⇄ ARR
  • SaaS Physics stores recurring state internally in ARR in v1. MRR is an exact presentation transformation (ARR / 12). MRR and ARR are two reporting bases for the same recurring economic state; switching basis changes no economic result.
  • MRR/ARR are recurring run-rate state measures. Revenue, gross profit and FCF are period financial flows and therefore do not toggle — they stay labelled · this month in both bases.
  • The default is MRR: the engine steps monthly, and New/Expansion/Leakage are monthly state movements, so a monthly basis matches the simulation's own resolution.
Reality vs measurement
  • The business exists first. KPIs are measurements taken from it — a KPI definition must never become an economic law.
  • The engine\u2019s coefficients are not the KPIs of similar name. A 90% persistence coefficient measures as 89.56% R12M GRR, and that gap is the within-period interaction of decay and expansion, not an error.
  • Two layers, kept apart: the economic engine, and the measurement layer that observes it.
Homogeneous Control World
  • The default has flat transition laws: age carries no differential dynamics.
  • Under homogeneous laws, cohort vintage affects provenance and capital history but does not create differential forward ARR dynamics.
  • With same-month acquisition (lag 0) it reduces exactly to ARR(t+1) = g\u00b7ARR(t) + N, verified to 4\u00d710\u207b\u00b9\u2075 against the engine. Under an acquisition lag the reduced form does not hold; under a capacity it does, with a smaller constant N.
  • This is a control case, not a claim that real retention is flat.
State-dependent illustrative world
  • Used only where explicitly selected — Scenario 6 and nowhere else.
  • It assigns different persistence and expansion to three age bands. That profile is a user assumption about a world, never a claimed law of SaaS.
  • It exists to show what can occur if transition behaviour varies with cohort maturity. It is not evidence that it does.
Major current boundaries
  • #10 Acquisition is linear and unbounded in S&M while acquisition capacity is off. With a capacity set (v1.2) the response saturates and the model shows average and marginal CAC — but it still declares no optimum. This is why no scenario here is "spend more".
  • #14 Expansion carries no separately modelled cost while expansion realisation cost is 0. With a cost set (v1.1) expansion ARR is priced; what the cost buys is not modelled.
  • #15 FCF = EBITA. No working capital, deferred revenue, tax or capex.
  • #16 R&D has no modelled effect on retention or expansion — which is why it is a fixed cost here, not a lever.
  • #17 Acquisition is same-month while the lag is 0. With a lag set (v1.3) spend sits in an explicit pending state before its cohort exists; spend whose ARR matures beyond M60 stays pending.
  • #18 Leakage is one number; there are no customers, so churn and contraction cannot be separated.
  • #21 There is no price.
Three mechanisms, each nullable (v1.1–v1.3)
  • Expansion realisation cost — € per €1 of expansion ARR. A cost line between gross profit and EBITA. Touches Cash, never ARR, GRR or NRR.
  • Acquisition capacity — New ARR = S&M ÷ (CAC + S&M ÷ capacity). A bound on an optimistic mechanism, not a growth benefit. Off = the linear law.
  • Acquisition lag — spend at month t creates its cohort at t + lag, through a pending state the engine actually carries. Changes when, never how much per euro.
  • With all three at their null setting the engine reproduces the v1.0 world exactly (the ALL-NULL release gate).
Customer physics (v2 Gate A, nullable)
  • Logo retention — the share of customers that survive twelve months. Departing customers take the cohort's average revenue with them.
  • Contraction — how much surviving customers shrink. Leakage = lost logos + contraction; persistence = L × (1 − C), derived, not set.
  • ARR per new logo — how many customers a euro of New ARR brings. Moves customers and ARPA, never ARR.
  • With Logo retention off the engine reproduces the v1.3 world exactly, every field (the ALL-NULL-V13 gate). Scenario 10 shows two worlds with identical ARR and NRR and different customers.
Monetization physics (v2 Gate B, nullable)
  • Components — each customer pays a platform fee (fixed) plus usage (penetration × units × price). Revenue is customers × components, recomputed every month: one source of truth.
  • Expansion — price growth, usage growth to a cap, adoption to a cap. The generic coefficient is bypassed; expansion saturates.
  • Contraction — reaches usage only, so dollar persistence is emergent from the mix. Scenario 12 shows identical customers and laws with different GRR.
  • Off = the customer world exactly. Scenario 11 shows the same customers priced as a coefficient and as components.
Cash physics (v2 Gate C, nullable)
  • Billings = revenue + Δdeferred revenue, by construction. Contracts are invoiced per billing term, in advance or in arrears; acquisition cohorts at birth, the opening book staggered.
  • Collections = billings shifted by the collection delay; receivables in between. Costs are cash when incurred.
  • Cash FCF = collections − cash costs = EBITA + Δdeferred − Δreceivables. EBITA never changes; only the cash path does.
  • Off = FCF = EBITA, the v1 world exactly. Scenario 13 shows the same P&L billed annually in advance.
Hypotheses (v2 Gate D, none by default)
  • An intervention is an object — target, effect, value, decision month, lag, duration, one-off and monthly cost — never a coefficient. The engine reads the law in force each month from lawAt(t).
  • Its cost is its own P&L line, booked from the decision month; cash when incurred.
  • Provenance — every month records which hypotheses were in force and what each changed; every cohort records the hypotheses in force when its spend was committed.
  • Bounds before benefits — a hypothesis cannot switch a layer on or change the billing policy, and is rejected up front if any month's resolved law leaves the engine's domain. Scenario 14 shows a retention programme.
What each visual earns its place by
  • Cohort strata \u2014 the historical composition and provenance of today\u2019s recurring state.
  • Capital-recovery track \u2014 the elapsed time between acquisition investment and economic recovery, so CAC payback becomes a distance rather than a number.
  • System map \u2014 a model audit: the causal topology the engine contains, with \u2298 marking links it does not.
  • A fourth candidate, an intra-month bridge animation, established no unique percept and was rejected. It is preserved in the research archive, not shipped.
Illustrative company throughout. No real company data is connected, and no valuation, multiple or embedded value is computed. The engine is deliberately a portable synthetic methodology: translating a real company into an opening state would be the job of a separate adapter, which does not exist and is out of scope.
