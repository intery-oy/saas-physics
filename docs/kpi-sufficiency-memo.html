When are SaaS KPIs sufficient statistics?

State, transition history, and hidden economic information in a deterministic cohort engine.

MODEL v0.3, frozen INTEGRITY 35/35 RESEARCH CHECKS 17/17 NEW PHYSICS none

01 · Executive finding

The retention metrics are not incomplete. They are uninformative.

Headline

Headline SaaS KPIs need not be sufficient statistics for future installed-base economics when transition laws depend on hidden cohort state — and where they fail, the failure is localised entirely in the retention ratios.

Two portfolios can report identical ARR, identical R12M GRR, identical R12M expansion and identical R12M NRR — to 0.00e+0, exactly, not to a tolerance — while the forward gross profit of their existing ARR differs by 23.4%. Both are governed by the same law set: they receive the same band array object, so per-portfolio calibration is structurally impossible.

That much was expected. Three results were not.

Retention history buys nothing. Given only R12M GRR and expansion, thirty-six months of history leaves exactly the ambiguity a single snapshot left: 4.51%, unchanged at every window. The entire collapse to zero is achieved by the ARR path, which does it alone.

The ambiguity closes fast, and cheaply. Twelve months of ARR history closes it completely, and it stays closed under measurement error at twenty-four months and beyond. The remedy is not cohort disclosure — it is a number every company already reports.

Observational and economic ambiguity are different quantities. At 24 and 36 months the state is still not identifiable, yet nothing economically relevant remains hidden. Had the study reported identifiability alone it would have concluded that KPI history fails at every window.

Why. Under a non-monotone tenure profile a young cohort and a mature cohort transition identically, so no retention ratio can separate them — by construction, at any window length. What separates them is the shape of the ARR trajectory: a base being replenished by recent acquisition traces a different path than one that is not, and it is that path which reveals how much ARR still faces the risk window.

PASSED. The observations agree exactly, not within a tolerance. Both portfolios reference the same band array object in memory, so per-portfolio calibration is not merely absent — it is structurally impossible in this construction.

Scope condition, load-bearing. The result depends on a non-monotone retention profile: bands 1 and 3 share coefficients, which is what lets two different ages report identical KPIs. Under a monotone tenure curve the (GRR, expansion) signature is nearly one-dimensional in age and an exact matched construction does not exist. That earlier infeasibility is the other half of this finding: under monotone tenure laws the KPI set may be very nearly sufficient. The model cannot say which regime real companies are in.

04 · Definitions

Two measures, to retire the vague language

FIBC-60 — Forward Installed-Base Contribution

FIBC60(s) = Σ (t=1..60) GrossProfit_t of the Time-0 installed base. Only cohorts existing at T0 count; later acquisitions are excluded, so the measure is a property of the state, not of future acquisition policy.

No discounting. No terminal value. No multiple. It is not called "value." "Economic content", "ARR quality" and "forward value" are retired.

The historical 26.5% was an artefact of its denominator

SKSG — the SaaS KPI Sufficiency Gap

The maximum FIBC-60 spread among observationally identical states, over the symmetric midpoint. Chosen because it is order-invariant — swapping the states cannot change the number, so the metric cannot be inflated by choosing a baseline, a choice worth 5.6 percentage points on the flagship pair alone.

Every SKSG figure discloses its KPI set, history window, law set, state domain, forward horizon and normalisation. It is a searched maximum over a finite declared domain — a lower bound on true ambiguity, never a theorem.

05 · Method

Two structural facts that shaped the experiment

The observation window ages the base. A cohort seeded at month 0 with age a has age a + T0 at T0. With T0 = 47 every directly-seeded euro is at least 47 months old and sits in the terminal band — where composition is provably inert: seeded ages 50, 80 and 120 all give FIBC-60 per euro of 4.778638602, identical to nine decimals.

So the hidden state is recent acquisition. The only Time-0 state that can differ economically is ARR young enough to still face the discriminating band, and with T0 ≥ 12 that ARR cannot have been seeded — it must have been acquired. Under a constant acquisition rate that mass is pinned by the rate itself, so the domain has to contain acquisition paths that are not constant. It does: two phases, joined losslessly (max |ΔKPI| = 0.00e+0 across all 36 measurement dates against a single continuous run).

T0 is held fixed at 47 for every window. Letting it move with W would confound more observation with more ageing — two different effects that both reduce ambiguity.

The snapshot ambiguity is structural, not noise — no amount of measurement precision reduces it. Longer history does not merely identify the state; it identifies it robustly. And note what carries that robustness: the identifying observable is the ARR path, a balance, reported precisely. The noisy observables were never doing the work.

No Jacobian, rank or condition number is reported. Those objects require a differentiable inversion of a continuous state vector; the domain here is a declared finite grid evaluated exhaustively. Computing one would have been decoration.

06 · Limits

Model result, not empirical claim

What the model proves

  • Headline KPIs need not be sufficient statistics when transition laws depend on hidden cohort state — by exact counterexample, not approximation.
  • Where they fail, the failure is carried entirely by the retention ratios.
  • Under homogeneous laws compression is safe: 256 states collapse to one observational class with SKSG 0.00%.
  • Hidden state here has a finite lifetime — once every euro reaches the terminal band, composition is inert.

What it does not prove

  • That any real SaaS base has non-monotone tenure dependence. The entire result is conditional on it.
  • That real diligence processes are systematically wrong.
  • That cohort disclosure is always necessary — here, twelve months of ARR history sufficed.
  • That 23.4% is empirically typical.
  • That the searched maximum is the true maximum. A richer state space could only raise SKSG, never lower it.

Implications, stated as conditionals

If a base has non-monotone tenure-dependent retention, then a KPI snapshot cannot bound forward installed-base gross profit, and three years of retention metrics will not help. The cheap fix is the ARR path, not cohort disclosure — a materially smaller ask, and one every company already reports.

Where retention is genuinely tenure-flat, none of this applies and the standard KPI set is sufficient for forward ARR dynamics. Establishing which regime a company is in is therefore the prior question — and it is empirical.

What would move it

  • Do real SaaS bases show a mid-life retention trough? Load-bearing, and directly measurable from any company's own cohort data.
  • How large is tenure dispersion in practice? SKSG scales with the gap between band coefficients; 94/78 is an assumption.
  • What is the real reporting precision of GRR and NRR? ±0.1pp is a stipulation.
  • Does the finite lifetime of hidden state survive a finer band structure, or age-continuous laws?

07 · Gate

Decision

Recommended next move

Proceed to acquisition-nonlinearity design. The state-sufficiency thesis is not weak — it survived the same-world gate exactly and now sits inside a metric rather than an anecdote. But it has been answered as far as this engine can answer it, and the binding constraint has moved to the fact that acquisition is linear and unbounded, so the model can always buy growth and cannot say stop. That is a bound on an existing optimistic mechanism, not a new claimed benefit — the lower evidence bar. Not implemented in this iteration.
