What you are looking at

Every visual property below is bound to a number the simulation actually computes. Nothing moves decoratively.

The mass
  • Height = ARR. The recurring stock, at every moment of its life.
  • Strata = cohorts. Each band is one acquisition vintage; the opening base is the bottom layer and every month deposits a new one on top, so vertical position is age.
  • Colour runs oldest–darkest to newest–lightest, so a vintage stays traceable for its whole life.
  • Brightness = gross margin. Same mass, dimmer, means the same recurring revenue carrying less economic content.
  • A brighter top edge marks a cohort in its first two months — new business entering.
Below the line
  • The shadow = cumulative leakage. Every euro the installed base has lost and had to replace. It only ever deepens.
  • Deliberately calm slate, not red: leakage is physics, not an alarm.
  • The keel = cash. Above the seam is cash generated, below it is cash consumed.
Time
  • Left of the playhead is realised: solid, filled, already true.
  • Right of the playhead is projected: unfilled outline, because it has not happened.
  • The playhead is now. Pause anywhere and the right-hand column describes the company at that instant.
Two futures
  • Solid, warm = Experiment, the scenario you steer.
  • Dashed, cool = Base, the frozen reference. It never changes.
  • When the assumptions match, the dashed line lies exactly on the mass. Move a force and the mass pulls away from its own ghost — the future itself has changed.
The capital loop — concept study
  • The drop at a cohort's acquisition month = the acquisition cost stamped on it when it was created. Capital leaving.
  • Below the surface = capital still out: acquisition cost minus that cohort's cumulative gross profit.
  • The crossing = payback — the first month cumulative gross profit reaches the acquisition cost. The horizontal distance from the drop to the crossing is the payback time.
  • Above the surface = gross profit beyond the acquisition cost attributed to that cohort. Not profit, value or ROI.
  • GP by vintage shows which historical cohorts produce today's gross profit — the delayed return on capital deployed months or years ago.
  • The dashed cool curve is the same cohort under Base. When retention or margin changes, the two recovery curves separate even though the money spent was identical.
The Pulse — Flow layer
  • Nine steps, in the engine's own order. Opening → leakage → expansion → new cohort → closing → revenue → margin → operating → cash. The order is the interaction; the formula for each step is on the transport bar.
  • Two columns, three bands. The bands between them are the sums of the movements marked inside the strata — and the bridge residual is shown, so the identity proves itself.
  • Expansion applies to RETAINED ARR, not opening. Revenue comes from the midpoint of opening and closing, marked with a dashed line — not from closing alone.
  • Three declared scales, printed on screen: stock, ARR movement, and € per month. One month of flow is about 1% of the stock, so they cannot share a scale honestly.
  • Controls sit on the laws they govern — persistence under the leakage band, gross margin at the revenue split.
  • Delta Pulse renders Experiment − Base only. Base becomes zero, the delta becomes the whole canvas, and nothing is exaggerated to make it visible.
Disclosed boundaries
  • This is an economic attribution. Gross profit does not literally flow back to the euro of S&M that was spent.
  • FCF = EBITA. No working capital, deferred revenue, tax or capex — so cash timing is pessimistic for a fast grower.
  • Expansion carries no modelled acquisition cost. An explicit current physics assumption, not a claim about SaaS.
  • One uniform gross margin applies to every cohort, so GP-by-vintage is the ARR mix scaled by a constant. It carries no information the strata do not already carry.
  • Nothing here is discounted, and no valuation or embedded value is computed.
